## Bold Markdown Subheading Style Rules

Use these rules when adding, renaming, merging, splitting, or reordering bold Markdown subheadings in a RecordPrep organized summary text file. These rules apply only to subheadings, not to hearing date lines, Quick point lines, topic sentences, report-title lines, or sourced body sentences.

Write every organized-summary subheading as a separate bold Markdown line:

`**Short Descriptive Topic Heading**`

## General Rules

- Write subheadings as short descriptive topic headings, not full sentences.
- Bold the entire subheading line with Markdown `**...**`.
- Do not use Markdown heading markers such as `#`, `##`, or `###`.
- Use Title Case.
- Capitalize major words.
- Do not capitalize short articles, conjunctions, or prepositions unless they are the first or last word.
- Keep each subheading limited to the related facts or procedural events in that section.
- Do not use a subheading to add, infer, summarize, or argue facts that do not appear in the source text.

## Dates

- If the source text provides a specific calendar date, write that date in long-form U.S. style.
- Convert numeric dates to long-form U.S. style.
- Example: `02/24/2024` becomes `February 24, 2024`.
- Do not convert relative, approximate, or vague time references into calendar dates.
- Preserve relative expressions such as `yesterday`, `today`, `tomorrow`, `last week`, and `earlier this month` as written.
- Preserve approximate date phrases from the source text unless the subheading requires the phrase to be normalized under these style rules.
- When writing a modifier before a month or year, keep the modifier and hyphenate it.
- Examples: `early-January 2025`, `mid-March 2024`, `late-May 2022`, and `late-2023`.
- Never invent, infer, calculate, or update a date that does not appear in the source text.

## Kinship Terms

- Use `Maternal` for the mother’s side of the family.
- Use `Paternal` for the father’s side of the family.
- Prefer `Maternal Grandmother`, `Maternal Grandfather`, `Paternal Grandmother`, or `Paternal Grandfather` over phrases such as `mother’s mother` or `father’s father`.

## Initials in Names

- If a person’s or child’s name appears as initials, add a period after each letter.
- Do not add spaces between initials.
- Examples:
  - `BR` becomes `B.R.`
  - `AL` becomes `A.L.`
  - `FP` becomes `F.P.`
  - `TWT` becomes `T.W.T.`

## Quotation Marks and Apostrophes

- Preserve quoted words exactly.
- Use curly single quotation marks, curly double quotation marks, and curly apostrophes in subheadings.
- Do not use straight single quotes, straight double quotes, or straight apostrophes.
- Do not output these prohibited characters:
  - U+0022 STRAIGHT DOUBLE QUOTATION MARK
  - U+0027 STRAIGHT APOSTROPHE
- Use these characters instead:
  - U+201C LEFT DOUBLE QUOTATION MARK
  - U+201D RIGHT DOUBLE QUOTATION MARK
  - U+2018 LEFT SINGLE QUOTATION MARK
  - U+2019 RIGHT SINGLE QUOTATION MARK, APOSTROPHE, OR CONTRACTION MARK
- Use U+2019 for possessives.
- Example: `Jesse’s Truck`.
- Use U+2019 for contractions.
- Example: `Tom Said It’s Going to Be a Wonderful Day`.
